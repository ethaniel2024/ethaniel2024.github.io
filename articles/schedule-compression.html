Schedule Compression and Performance in Basketball

Teams do not just win because they are better—they also win because their schedule is easier.

By Ethan Nelson

Overview

This project examines how travel distance, rest days, and game density affect basketball performance. Using game-level data, it estimates how scheduling factors influence win probability and how those effects accumulate across a season.

Key findings

  • Back-to-back games reduce win probability by approximately 6.6%.
  • Opponent strength is the dominant driver of individual game outcomes.
  • Schedule effects are modest game to game, but compound over a season.
  • Teams can gain or lose more than 100 wins across a sample period because of schedule differences.

Method

I constructed team schedules with dates, locations, and travel distances; engineered rest and density features; estimated opponent strength using net ratings; and modeled outcomes with opponent strength, travel, rest, and game density as predictors.

The framework translates directly to college basketball, where tournament structure, byes, and travel variation create similar constraints.
